Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for solving partial differential equations in geophysical applications. In this post, I discuss recent developments in applying PINNs to elastic wave modeling and full-waveform inversion problems.

## Key Challenges and Solutions

One of the main challenges in geophysical applications is the high-frequency nature of seismic waves. Traditional PINN approaches often struggle with these high-frequency components. Our recent work addresses this by:

1. **Multi-scale training strategies**: Starting with low-frequency problems and gradually increasing complexity
2. **Adaptive sampling techniques**: Focusing computational resources on regions with high gradients
3. **Physics-constrained loss functions**: Incorporating wave equation constraints directly into the training process

## Applications in Structural Health Monitoring

The integration of PINNs with IoT-based monitoring systems opens new possibilities for real-time structural health assessment. By combining edge computing with physics-informed models, we can achieve:

- Real-time wavefield reconstruction
- Automated anomaly detection
- Predictive maintenance capabilities

## Future Directions

Looking ahead, we're exploring:
- Operator learning approaches for faster inference
- Uncertainty quantification in PINN predictions
- Integration with traditional numerical methods
