I also spent four years building IoT sensor networks for environmental monitoring in East Africa. That work taught me how hard it is to measure the world, which is a big part of why I now study how to model it.

IN PLAIN WORDS

When you tap on a concrete block, sound waves travel through it. A crack or a weak spot changes how those waves move and echo. If we record the waves at the surface, we can try to work backwards and find where the damage is. This is called an inverse problem, and it is hard because many different insides can give almost the same signal.

I train neural networks that know the wave equation, so they can simulate the waves and also search for the hidden damage. Most of my tests so far use computer-generated data. The next step is real laboratory measurements.
